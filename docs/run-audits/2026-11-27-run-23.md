### Discovery and source grounding

WHO's 2026 *Guidelines for drinking-water quality*, Chapter 5 (pp. 75–80), distinguishes surveillance from supplier quality control, says surveillance must account for different supply types, and recommends well-designed surveys when extensive surveillance of all supplies is impractical. For dispersed small supplies, it describes rolling programmes using stratified-random or cluster sampling. Official source: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/water-safety-and-quality/dwq-guidelines-4/gdwq_4ed_with_third-addenda_surveillance.pdf

The current documentary evidence contains two source-reported 2023 percentages:
- JECM 2025 secondary analysis of National Food Agency monitoring data: 49.5%.
- Government of Georgia report submitted through the UNECE/WHO reporting system: 44.5%.

These are not established as the same indicator or sample frame. The government submission is not an independent UNECE/WHO survey. Neither numerator nor denominator, full sampling frame, exact water matrix, standard version, inclusion rule and analytical-method mapping were verified in the material used here. The five-percentage-point difference remains unresolved.

### Engineering

Added:
- `noema_core/water_comparability.py`
- `data/noema-006/water-indicator-observation.schema.json`
- `data/noema-006/2023-water-noncompliance-source-observations.json`

The comparator requires identical indicator definition, water matrix, standard/version, period, geography, sampling frame/design, inclusion rule and analytical method. It also validates numerator/denominator and the source-rounded percentage. Missing metadata yields `INSUFFICIENT_METADATA`; mismatched known fields yield `NOT_COMPARABLE`; absent counts yield `INSUFFICIENT_COUNTS`; a rate/count mismatch yields `SOURCE_RATE_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`. A successful result is descriptive only and always sets `population_inference_permitted=false`.

The source fixture preserves 49.5% and 44.5% with unknown fields as null. It does not compute their difference. The focused test set contains 12 tests and passed on both exact-head workflows. The full suite contained 469 tests with one skipped on both workflows.

### Interpretation and limits

**FACT:** the cited sources publish the two percentages and WHO guidance sets out the surveillance/sampling principles.  
**REPRODUCED RESULT:** synthetic fixtures demonstrate that the gate refuses incomplete metadata and only returns a descriptive comparison when definitions/counts reconcile.  
**INFERENCE:** a metadata-first gate reduces the risk of false trends or municipal rankings from unlike samples.  
**NOT ESTABLISHED:** the true reason for the 49.5%/44.5% discrepancy, a current national compliance rate, municipal risk ranking, population prevalence or water-safety classification.

No raw sample-level, household, health, private-well or operational records were added. No NFA/NEA API or field integration was performed.

### Discovery and methodology

The 2026 UNDRR/IFRC/ITU/WMO After-Event Review guidance evaluates monitoring, forecasting, warning dissemination, communication/coordination and response, using a learning-oriented, non-punitive approach: https://www.undrr.org/publication/documents-and-publications/after-event-review-aer-methodological-guidance-conducting

The WMO/UNDRR 2026 MHEWS report shows 136 countries reporting MHEWS but only 64 reporting all four pillars. It identifies disaster-risk knowledge as the weakest pillar (72 of 136) and says effectiveness, behavioural response and protective action need to complement capability counts: https://www.undrr.org/reports/global-status-mhews-2026

These sources justify separating the timing of receipt from receipt itself; they do not establish a Georgian warning-delay problem.

### Engineering

Extended:
- `noema_core/action_readiness.py`
- `data/noema-007/action-readiness-observation.schema.json`
- `tests/test_noema007_action_readiness.py`
- `data/research-portfolio.json`
- `research/protocols/NOEMA-007-timely-receipt-measurement.md`

Four optional, backward-compatible survey fields represent timing-assessed respondents, timely receipts, event-specific minimum lead seconds and the measurement basis. The validator requires all four together; timing-assessed respondents cannot exceed self-reported warning receipts; timely receipts cannot exceed timing assessments. The basis is restricted to respondent recall. Endpoint receipt counts remain a separate unit and cannot be interpreted as people reached. Legacy records without the fields remain valid and return null rather than zero.

The exact-head CI passed the focused checks and the full Python suite (464 tests, one skipped).

### Interpretation and limits

**FACT:** the AER guidance reviews the warning-to-response chain; WMO/UNDRR reports country-level MHEWS capabilities and an effectiveness-measurement gap.  
**INFERENCE:** receipt without timing can hide warnings that arrive too late to support the intended action.  
**HYPOTHESIS:** an event-specific minimum lead-time measure can improve the diagnostic value of action-readiness assessment.  
**UNTESTED:** the new field has not been piloted in Georgia; no actual warning-event, survey or protective-action data were used.

The metric is self-reported, not timestamp-verified. Minimum lead time must be approved by the competent authority for the specific hazard and action playbook. No causal impact, evacuation coverage or avoided-loss claim is made.
