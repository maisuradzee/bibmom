## DISCOVERY

### AquaSignal — Georgia water-data access

**FACT:** Article 32(3) of the Water Resources Management Law states that NEA provides quantitative and qualitative water-observation data to interested parties on request. Article 31(3) separately assigns state control of drinking-water quality and consumer information to NFA.

**FACT:** Government Resolution No.424 applies to fresh drinking-groundwater extraction wells deeper than 20 m, excludes wells drilled before 1 September 2026, and requires the driller to submit the well passport within 15 working days after drilling completion. NEA's 1 September implementation notice separately says household wells in residential plots are excluded. The passport includes owner identity/contact information and well coordinates; field/laboratory results are conditional on sampling.

**INFERENCE:** A lawful request route exists, but that does not prove an open API, unrestricted raw-data access, a response deadline or public reuse rights. NEA and NFA requests must be separate.

### NOEMA-007 — Georgia flood-risk governance

**FACT:** NEA's 25 November 2026 notice describes a two-day consultation for seven named river basins and says the wider programme targets multi-hazard risk-management measures for eleven basins.

**FACT:** Article 34 assigns six-year preliminary flood-risk assessment and flood-hazard mapping to NEA, and flood-risk maps for significant-risk areas to the Emergency Management Service. Competent agencies prepare flood-risk plans in agreement with the Emergency Management Service.

**INFERENCE:** The statutory role split creates a provenance requirement. A hazard map is not a risk map; neither is a live warning; receipt, comprehension, feasibility and verified action are distinct outcomes.
