### Cross-architecture evidence

The merged manifest is `data/noema-005/cross-architecture-reduction-manifest-2026-11-27.json`. It records seven workflow artifacts with SHA-256 values, including native OpenMP, x86-64/ARM64 accumulator and four Python-thread runtime cells.

Native experiment:

- `long double` mantissa: 64 bits on x86-64 and 113 bits on ARM64.
- Full Neumaier state: 40/40 correctly rounded cases on each architecture.
- Exact superaccumulator: 40/40 on each architecture.
- Naive partials: 0/40 on each architecture.
- Pairwise tree: 0/40 on each architecture.
- `long double`: 20/40 on x86-64 and 40/40 on ARM64.

Python-thread experiment:
- 48 configurations per environment; three repetitions and one warm-up.
- In the manifest's aggregate `all_environments` accuracy record, full Neumaier state is 48/48, scalar Neumaier partial merge is 42/48 and naive scalar is 0/48. The manifest does not expose a separate per-environment accuracy breakdown in this summary, so no per-cell accuracy claim is made.
- Median wall speedup for the full Neumaier state remained below 1.0 in all four runner/runtime cells: 0.696 (macOS GIL), 0.742 (Ubuntu GIL), 0.707 (Ubuntu free-threaded), 0.697 (macOS free-threaded).
- Free-threaded CPU/wall ratios exceeded one in two environments, but this did not translate into net wall-time speedup on this workload.
- Median `tracemalloc` allocation was approximately 25 KB in GIL-enabled cells and 277 KB in free-threaded cells. This is not RSS, native allocator usage or DRAM bandwidth.

### Interpretation and limits

**FACT:** the committed manifest records the counts and workflow provenance above.  
**REPRODUCED RESULT:** repository CI reran the frozen analysis and schema/tests.  
**INFERENCE:** a complete compensation state or exact accumulator is more reliable than naive/pairwise partial reduction for these tested cancellation cases.  
**NOT ESTABLISHED:** general performance superiority, production workload behaviour, hardware-independent accuracy guarantees, energy use or memory-bandwidth behaviour.

All measurements used hosted GitHub runners and synthetic workloads. Five native and three Python repetitions are not a stable performance distribution. Raw Actions artifacts have 30-day retention; the SHA-256 manifest preserves provenance but not binary bytes. Next step: independent physical x86-64 and ARM64 devices, at least 20 repetitions per configuration, per-thread clocks/affinity, RSS/native allocation, DRAM bandwidth and representative workloads. External numerical-method review remains required.

## 2. AquaSignal — public access to drinking-water information

PR: [#128 — AquaSignal public-information/accessibility addendum](https://github.com/maisuradzee/bibmom/pull/128)  
Current head: `272f3a25e402ae26da259f90bd395c3d6af5aa0b`  
State: Draft, mergeable=true; exact-head Quality CI [45964721254](https://github.com/maisuradzee/bibmom/actions/runs/45964721254) SUCCESS.

### New discovery

The Public Defender of Georgia's 2025 special report states that lack of information about drinking-water quality was one of the main challenges observed during its monitoring work. It reports visits to five regions, 35 villages and 15 municipalities and recommends a unified public-information platform/database plus supplementary channels. The visits are not a nationally representative probability sample and do not establish a national prevalence rate.

The JECM 2025 article explicitly describes a secondary analysis of National Food Agency monitoring data for 2020–2023 and reports 49.5% broad non-compliance among 2023 samples. The Government of Georgia's report submitted through the UNECE/WHO reporting system reports 44.5% broad non-compliance for 2023. The five-percentage-point difference remains unresolved; the government submission is not an independent UNECE/WHO survey. The 2024 E. coli-specific table remains a separate indicator context.

### Innovation proposal

The PR proposes an authority-owned disclosure layer, not an autonomous water-safety score:
- keep potable network, groundwater, surface water and bottled-water matrices separate;
- require source authority, sample frame, sample date, analyte, unit, method, lab/accreditation, QA, standard/version, denominator and revision metadata;
- show missing/stale/withdrawn results as unknown, never safe;
- provide authority-approved online and supplementary dissemination channels;
- preserve correction history and provenance;
- prohibit personal GPS, household/health records, exact private-well coordinates and NOEMA-generated safe/unsafe labels.

## 5. Separate CI blocker — HadCRUT5 coverage reconstruction

The separate workflow [45963513863](https://github.com/maisuradzee/bibmom/actions/runs/45963513863) failed on main snapshot `fa711306d5eb588d1c9e97b207d8358c879fd04a`. Its artifact reports:
- annual coverage-sigma RMSE: 0.0003516123 °C;
- mean absolute difference: 0.0003010265 °C;
- max absolute difference: 0.0006968631 °C, above the 0.0005 °C threshold;
- correlation: 0.9718820223;
- published mean sigma 0.0046862566 °C versus computed 0.0044119317 °C;
- area-fraction max difference only 3.44e-8.

Seven annual values exceed the absolute-difference threshold and computed sigma is lower in each. The tolerance was not relaxed and downstream combined-trend claims must remain blocked until this is reconciled.

The primary HadCRUT5 supporting information states that coverage uncertainty uses ERA5 reference fields and the RMS of masked-minus-complete errors across reference years (pp. 9-10): https://hadleyserver.metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/HadCRUT5_supporting_information_accepted.pdf. The repository method uses the stated 1979-1990 monthly climatology and 40 reference years; the exact ERA5 source/version, regridding and published-series alignment still require independent audit. This workflow was not rerun by the INFRA-only main merge because its relevant paths did not change.
