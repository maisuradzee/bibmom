# NOEMA — DISCOVERY · RESEARCH · INNOVATION

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**Status:** operating model proposal

NOEMA is not only a research archive. It is a problem-to-evidence-to-solution system. Its three directions are separate responsibilities in one traceable lifecycle.

## 1. NOEMA DISCOVERY

Purpose: identify real problems, unmet needs and early signals before they are treated as established findings.

Each candidate records:

- the affected people, place, system or environment;
- the observation and the date it was observed;
- source provenance, access class and reuse permission;
- potential scale and severity of human impact;
- urgency and plausible time-to-harm;
- evidence strength and competing explanations;
- actionability: whether a safe, testable next step exists;
- unknowns and data-access constraints.

Discovery must not use an opaque AI score as a substitute for evidence. Human-impact, urgency, evidence and actionability are kept as separate dimensions. A candidate with high impact but weak evidence is a research priority, not a confirmed emergency.

## 2. NOEMA RESEARCH

Purpose: test the discovery claim and reduce uncertainty.

Required sequence:

1. State a falsifiable question before examining outcome data where practicable.
2. Prefer primary sources, official datasets and peer-reviewed research.
3. Record exact versions, retrieval dates, licences and hashes where files are retained.
4. Separate training, calibration and test data; preserve a simple baseline.
5. Record null, negative and failed results.
6. Make the method independently reproducible.
7. Label every material claim as FACT, REPRODUCED_RESULT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS or UNTESTED_IDEA.

A documentary review is not an experiment. A synthetic test validates software behavior, not real-world performance.

## 3. NOEMA INNOVATION

Purpose: turn a sufficiently supported need into a safe, testable solution.

Every prototype must define:

- the user and decision it supports;
- the input data and permission basis;
- the output and the action it can trigger;
- failure modes, uncertainty and misuse cases;
- a human review or escalation boundary;
- a measurable pilot plan and a stop condition;
- cost, accessibility, maintainability and environmental impact.

A prototype is not a validated product. High-impact, medical, legal, financial or physical-world actions remain subject to authorized human review.

## 4. Lifecycle and gates

candidate → evidence review → experiment → prototype → pilot → independent validation → publication or retirement

A stage advances only when its acceptance criteria are met. A failed or inconclusive result remains visible and may send the work back to discovery or research. The system must not convert a hypothesis into a public alert or a prototype into a safety guarantee.

## 5. Prioritization

Keep a transparent vector rather than one opaque ranking:

- **Impact:** who could be harmed or helped, and how severely?
- **Urgency:** how quickly could the situation change?
- **Evidence:** how strong, independent and current are the sources?
- **Actionability:** is there a safe, feasible test or intervention?
- **Equity:** are vulnerable or underserved groups disproportionately affected?

The vector is a triage aid, not a probability of harm. Record the rationale and missing evidence beside it.

## 6. Operational autonomy

NOEMA may autonomously retrieve permitted public sources, normalize permitted data, run reproducible analysis, draft reports, create internal prototypes and run quality checks. It must fail closed on unknown source permissions and unknown action types. It must not bypass access controls, claim unearned legal rights, expose private material in the public repository, or make high-impact decisions without authorized review.

Routine work can be automated. Independent scientific review, permission grants, funding, physical intervention and final high-impact decisions cannot be manufactured by the software itself.

## 7. Public repository boundary

The repository is public, including branches and pull requests. Only material that is safe to publish may be committed. Drafts may be excluded from GitHub Pages, but they are still visible on GitHub. Private research material requires a genuinely private storage boundary; a public branch is not a private archive.
