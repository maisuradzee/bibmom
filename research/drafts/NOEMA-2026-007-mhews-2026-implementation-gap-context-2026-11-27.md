# NOEMA-007 — MHEWS 2026: coverage progress and the warning-to-action gap

- **ID:** NOEMA-2026-007
- **Status:** draft
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Evidence classification:** FACT / SOURCE-REPORTED ASSOCIATION / INFERENCE

## Research question

Does the presence of a multi-hazard early warning system adequately describe whether people are protected, or must measurement also cover the full chain from hazard knowledge through warning delivery, comprehension and feasible action?

## Primary-source findings

1. **FACT — WMO MHEWS 2026:** 136 countries reported multi-hazard early warning systems as of August 2026, a 143% increase since 2015. Fewer than half of reporting countries demonstrated capabilities across all four pillars. Source: https://public.wmo.int/resources/publication-series/global-status-multi-hazard-early-warning-systems/gsmhews-2026 (published 2026-11-11).
2. **REPRODUCED RESULT — existing NOEMA arithmetic:** the frozen country-level snapshot records 64 of 136 reporting countries with all four pillars, or 47.0588%. This is a country-capability ratio, not population coverage.
3. **SOURCE-REPORTED ASSOCIATION — UNDRR/WMO COP31 update:** countries with substantial-to-comprehensive MHEWS reportedly have nearly six times lower disaster-related death rates than countries with limited-to-moderate systems. Source: https://wmo.int/media/news/cop31-leaders-back-long-term-future-early-warnings-all (published 2026-11-12).
4. **SOURCE-REPORTED IMPLEMENTATION GAP:** the same WMO update reports that four in five people affected by a disaster in the previous five years received advance warning, while more than half of reporting countries still experience a breakdown somewhere between forecast and action.

## Interpretation

- **INFERENCE:** counting whether a country reports an MHEWS is not sufficient to measure whether exposed people receive, understand and can act on a warning.
- **INFERENCE:** the nearly sixfold mortality comparison is a cross-country association. It may reflect broader differences in governance, exposure, infrastructure, income, health systems and preparedness; it must not be presented as a causal effect of MHEWS alone.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** a warning-to-action evaluation should separately measure issued, delivered, received, understood, feasible, actioned and independently verified protective action, with distinct denominators and privacy-safe event-level aggregation.
- **UNTESTED IDEA:** a trusted local action-readiness layer could connect an official warning to pre-agreed playbooks, accessible communication channels, evacuation/transport constraints and social-protection resources without issuing duplicate official warnings.

## Method and limitations

This note re-opened the WMO 2026 publication page and the UNDRR/WMO COP31 update on 2026-11-27. It did not independently reprocess country-level MHEWS data or mortality microdata. The existing 64/136 arithmetic is reproduced from the repository's frozen source snapshot. No causal model, Georgia-specific estimate or local operational effectiveness estimate is claimed.

The published pages do not provide the country-level microdata required for independent causal analysis. A Georgian assessment still requires authorized event-level warning, delivery, comprehension, action-feasibility and after-action data with approved privacy controls and explicit denominators.
