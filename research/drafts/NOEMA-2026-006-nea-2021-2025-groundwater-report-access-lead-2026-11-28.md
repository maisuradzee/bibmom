## საკვლევი კითხვა

Can the newly announced National Environmental Agency (NEA) 2021-2025 groundwater-monitoring report and its nitrate-risk field methodology provide a traceable baseline for AquaSignal, and what evidence is needed before extracting sample-level or site-level conclusions?

## მოკლე შეჯამება

On 2026-11-26, the NEA announced that its Geological Department had prepared a hydrogeological report covering 2021-2025 monitoring results and observation-network development, plus a field methodology for identifying groundwater contaminated with nitrates or at risk of contamination. The official page links a public RAR archive.

This is a high-value official source-discovery result. The archive was not retrieved or inspected in this run; no sample counts, station counts, nitrate values, thresholds, locations or results are asserted. The source record is metadata-only.

## რატომ არის ეს კითხვა მნიშვნელოვანი?

Groundwater monitoring and nitrate-risk identification can help identify risks before they become persistent exposure problems. A five-year monitoring report may provide temporal and network context that is not available from the NFA's planned 2026 drinking-water sampling allocation. AquaSignal must keep groundwater, distributed drinking water, bottled water and food-control records as separate matrices until the source data dictionary proves comparability.

## არსებული ცოდნა და წყაროები

The NEA announcement says the report covers quantitative and qualitative groundwater characteristics, operation of the automatic monitoring system, processing/digitization of historical hydrogeological information, and development prospects for the observation network. It separately says a practical field methodology was prepared for identifying groundwater contaminated with nitrates or at risk of nitrate contamination.

A Georgian law dated 2026-06-24 sets 2027-09-01 milestones for approval of relevant water-resource-management methodologies and identification of groundwater nitrate-contaminated or at-risk zones. The NEA announcement that a field method was prepared does not prove that the method has been formally approved or that statutory zone identification is complete.

## WHO 2026 drinking-water comparator — not Georgian law

The WHO's current 2026 fourth edition incorporating the first, second and third addenda was published on 2026-06-17. Its nitrate/nitrite chemical fact sheet gives:
- **Nitrate:** 50 mg/L as nitrate ion.
- **Nitrite:** 3 mg/L as nitrite ion.
- **Combined occurrence:** the sum of the nitrate and nitrite concentration-to-guideline ratios should not exceed 1:
  `C_nitrate/GV_nitrate + C_nitrite/GV_nitrite <= 1`.
- **Conversion factors:** 1 mg/L nitrate ion = 0.226 mg/L nitrate-nitrogen; 1 mg/L nitrite ion = 0.304 mg/L nitrite-nitrogen.

The same fact sheet lists its **assessment date as 2016**. The 2026 publication date therefore must not be described as a new 2026 toxicological re-derivation of these values.

The guideline values are global health-based guidance for **drinking-water**, not Georgian statutory limits. They are not automatically applicable to raw groundwater or every monitoring point; first confirm whether a sample represents water intended for human consumption, the analytical reporting basis (ion versus nitrogen), and the applicable Georgian legal standard.

WHO identifies bottle-fed infants as the most sensitive subgroup and notes that microbial contamination and gastrointestinal infection can increase methaemoglobinaemia risk when nitrate is present. It also warns that excessive boiling can concentrate nitrate; boiling is not a nitrate-removal treatment.

- **FACT:** the 2026 WHO edition contains the 50 mg/L nitrate, 3 mg/L nitrite and combined-ratio guidance; the fact sheet assessment date is 2016.
- **INFERENCE:** these values can serve as an international drinking-water comparator after the sample matrix and intended use are confirmed.
- **UNTESTED IDEA:** a future AquaSignal rule could calculate the combined ratio from paired nitrate/nitrite measurements with explicit units and source provenance.
- **Not claimed:** Georgian legal compliance, raw-groundwater exceedance, local exposure or a health outcome.

## Historical NEA groundwater nitrate baseline — 2016–2017 only

The ENI SEIS II East / European Environment Agency indicator C11 states that the data were supplied by Georgia's National Environmental Agency. Groundwater monitoring had resumed in 2013; the network had 45 sites, but the 2016–2017 analysis had data for 34 sites. Sampling frequency was twice per year, and the reported present-state value was the average of annual mean concentrations for 2016–2017.

Published summary:
- All 34 available site averages were below the cited national threshold of 50 mg NO3/L.
- The indicator says all except one monitoring site had an average above 10 mg NO3/L. Given 34 sites, that is 33/34 by subtraction from the published statement.
- All western monitoring sites were below 1 mg NO3/L; the page text does not state the western-site count.
- Concentrations were generally lower in western Georgia. The indicator suggests less agriculture as a possible explanation, but this is not a causal finding.
- The indicator specifies no uncertainty estimate and does not provide raw site/sample values in the page text.

This is a **historical, aggregated baseline**, not a 2026 measurement, not a population-weighted exposure survey and not proof that all current drinking-water supplies are safe. It is useful for designing a 2021–2025/2026 comparison only if the NEA archive provides compatible station identifiers, sample dates, analytes, units, methods and QA/QC.

Source: https://eni-seis.eionet.europa.eu/east/indicators/c11-nutrients-in-freshwater-of-georgia/ (published 2020-01-20; last modified 2020-02-07; data provider NEA).

## Georgian legal crosswalk — Decree №58/2014

The current Matsne page for Government Decree №58 of 2014-01-15 sets:
- **Nitrate:** 50 mg/L as NO3-.
- **Nitrite:** 0.2 mg/L as NO2-.

The regulation applies to natural or treated water used for drinking, food preparation and domestic purposes, and water used in food/food-product production. It excludes medicinal mineral waters, medical/special-purpose water, certain small private individual sources (<10 m³/day, fewer than 50 people, not in a public/commercial network), and natural mineral water with total mineralization above 1500 mg/L. Supplier monitoring is required and testing is to be performed by an accredited independent laboratory.

This matters because the WHO 2026 drinking-water fact sheet gives a nitrite guideline of 3 mg/L as nitrite ion, while Georgian Decree №58/2014 sets 0.2 mg/L as NO2- for water within its scope. **The WHO value must not replace the Georgian legal value for compliance.** The decree's Article 2(21) has a general sum-of-ratios clause for substances normalized by the same limiting sign; this note does not interpret it as a specific nitrate/nitrite combined-ratio formula.

Official legal source: https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/2196792 (accessed 2026-11-28).
