# NOEMA-2026-007 — Georgia flood-risk law: institutional roles and measurement boundary

- **ID:** NOEMA-2026-007 (continuation; no new portfolio stream)
- **Status:** draft
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Scope:** Georgia river-basin planning, flood-risk governance and warning-to-action evaluation
- **Related work:** [Georgia river-basin multi-hazard readiness](NOEMA-2026-007-georgia-basin-readiness-2026-11-27.md)

## Research question

How should NOEMA evaluate the path from official flood-risk assessment and warning to feasible protective action in Georgia while preserving the statutory roles of competent public authorities?

## Executive finding

Georgia's Water Resources Management Law gives a more precise institutional structure for flood-risk evidence than a generic “early-warning system” label suggests. Article 34 distinguishes:
- NEA's preliminary flood-risk assessment and flood-hazard mapping;
- the Emergency Management Service's flood-risk mapping for areas identified as having potentially significant flood risk;
- competent agencies' flood-risk management plans, coordinated with the Emergency Management Service.

This flood-specific legal framework must not be conflated with the wider multi-hazard programme. NEA's 25 November 2026 consultation concerned seven named river basins, while the announcement says the wider climate-risk programme aims to develop measures for eleven basins and covers seven hazard types. Seven consultation basins divided by eleven programme-target basins is **not** a valid implementation or coverage percentage.

## FACT — official source evidence

### 1. Current multi-hazard planning context

NEA reported a two-day consultation on 25 November 2026 covering plans for:
- East Georgia: Alazani, Iori, Mtkvari and Khrami-Ktsia;
- West Georgia: Chorokhi-Acharistskali, Enguri and Rioni.

The same notice says the broader programme aims to develop multi-hazard risk-management and mitigation measures for eleven river basins and lists floods, debris flows, avalanches, landslides, droughts, hailstorms and strong winds. It also says the meeting reviewed DRR/DRM structures, agency responsibilities and coordination mechanisms.

**Boundary:** a consultation is evidence of planning activity, not proof that a plan has been formally approved, funded, operationalised or tested.

### 2. Statutory flood-risk responsibilities

Article 34 of the Law of Georgia on Water Resources Management states that:
- NEA performs a preliminary flood-risk assessment for all river-basin units every six years and identifies areas with potentially significant flood risk (Article 34(1));
- NEA prepares flood-hazard maps; the Emergency Management Service prepares flood-risk maps for areas identified as potentially significantly at risk (Article 34(2));
- maps distinguish low- and high-probability scenarios, including a scenario of at least one event in 100 years, and identify the population, economic activity and environmental damage potentially at risk (Article 34(2));
- flood-risk management plans include flood forecasting and early-warning systems and are reflected in river-basin/sub-basin management plans (Article 34(3));
- competent state agencies prepare plans in agreement with the Emergency Management Service (Article 34(4)).

The law's provisions on water monitoring (Article 32) separately assign state water monitoring to NEA and require it to provide quantitative/qualitative observation data to interested parties on request. This is a potential data-access route, not proof of an open endpoint.

## INFERENCE — what can and cannot be measured

The statutory split creates a provenance requirement for any future NOEMA evaluation:
- a hazard map is not a risk map;
- a risk map is not a live forecast or warning;
- a warning endpoint is not proof that an exposed person received the warning;
- receipt is not proof of comprehension;
- comprehension is not proof that protective action was feasible;
- reported action is not independently verified action;
- verified action is not, by itself, a causal estimate of avoided loss.

NOEMA should not create, amend or republish official hazard/risk maps as authoritative products. It can evaluate publicly authorised versions and the downstream communication/action chain, with the responsible authority's approval.

## HYPOTHESIS

For one authorised basin and one flood scenario, a versioned chain linking the statutory hazard-map source, risk-map source, approved flood-risk plan, official warning event and after-action record could reveal where readiness breaks down without duplicating official decision-making.

This remains untested because no approved local map package, event-level warning dataset or after-action record was available for this review.

## UNTESTED IDEA — safe evaluation design

Use a versioned, authority-approved evidence record with:
- basin/sub-basin identifier and map/plan version;
- hazard type and scenario/return-period label as published by the competent authority;
- map owner, issuing authority, publication date and source digest;
- definition and date of the exposed-population denominator;
- warning-event identifier, issue time, validity window, geographic scope and revision history;
- approved action-playbook identifier and responsible institution;
- separate denominators for channel delivery, timely human receipt, comprehension, feasible action and verified action;
- after-action review and corrective-action closure status.

Use only privacy-reviewed aggregate outputs. Suppress small cells under an approved policy; a default cell-size floor alone is not an anonymisation guarantee. Do not collect message bodies, personal device identifiers or household-level coordinates in the public repository.

## Reproducibility and limitations

This is a documentary legal/governance analysis. No flood-risk map, local plan, warning-event record, survey or field outcome was downloaded or analysed. The 25 November consultation notice does not publish plan-approval status, map payloads, exposed-population denominators or warning effectiveness. Article 34 is flood-specific; it must not be presented as a complete statutory allocation for every hazard in the broader multi-hazard programme.

## Conclusion

The next NOEMA-007 step is not to build another warning service. It is to obtain the authorised, versioned flood-hazard maps, flood-risk maps, basin plans and warning/after-action aggregates from their respective owners, then evaluate delivery, comprehension, feasibility and verified action as separate stages.
