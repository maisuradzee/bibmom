Accuracy across all four reports: Neumaier state **192/192**, scalar Neumaier **168/192**, naive scalar **0/192**. Process CPU/wall >1.05 occurred in **216/288** free-threaded samples and **0/288** GIL-enabled samples.

The two Azure free-threaded reports both show Neumaier-state median speedup above 1.0 at 2, 4 and 8 workers; the 1-worker case remains below 1.0. The single matched Azure workflow pair shows 0.7809× GIL-enabled versus 1.3172× free-threaded Neumaier-state median speedup.

## INFERENCE

The results are promising for this synthetic workload: the two Azure free-threaded repeats preserve exact-reference accuracy and show median wall-time speedup at 2/4/8 workers. This is stronger than the earlier ARM64 result, where all median speedups remained below 1.0. It does not yet establish a general free-threaded advantage: only one GIL/free-threaded Azure pair is matched by runner family, the second GIL report is AWS, and every configuration has only three measured repetitions.

## HYPOTHESIS

The larger speedup on x86-64 Azure than on ARM64 hosted runners may reflect architecture, runner provisioning, scheduler, cache or workload effects. The current experiment does not isolate those causes.

## UNTESTED IDEA

Run at least 20 measured repetitions on fixed, independently controlled x86-64 and ARM64 machines; pin CPU model, microcode, affinity, OS image and power policy; add RSS/native allocator, DRAM bandwidth, per-thread CPU clocks and confidence intervals. Compare multiple chunk sizes and tree-reduction strategies.

## LIMITATIONS

- GitHub-hosted VMs are not physical-device or external-laboratory replication.
- Three repetitions per configuration are insufficient for robust uncertainty estimates.
- The deterministic cancellation inputs are synthetic, not representative production workloads.
- tracemalloc does not measure full process RSS, native allocator overhead or DRAM bandwidth.
- The second workflow's GIL job ran on AWS while its free-threaded job ran on Azure; do not compare those two runtime medians causally.
- The first workflow's Azure GIL/free pair uses the same cloud backend and runner label but separate hosted VMs, not a single controlled physical host.
- CPU/wall >1.05 indicates aggregate CPU consumption above wall time; it is not itself a speedup or throughput metric.
