## საკვლევი კითხვა

What does Georgia's 2026 preliminary flood-risk assessment decree establish, what evidence must still be produced before the first national assessment, and which parts of the early-warning chain remain outside this legal framework?

## Human benefit and priority

The framework can help identify river-basin areas where future flood-hazard mapping and risk-management measures should be prioritized. Potential beneficiaries include residents, local authorities, emergency services, infrastructure operators and vulnerable ecosystems. The benefit is prospective until the methodology and first national assessment are completed and published.

## FACT

Government Decree №440 of 8 September 2026 applies from 1 September 2026 and covers all river-basin districts in Georgia. The National Environment Agency is responsible for developing the preliminary-assessment methodology, carrying out and updating assessments, identifying potentially significant flood-risk areas, collecting relevant hydrological, meteorological, hydraulic, geomorphological and geological information, and maintaining national databases.

The assessment may consider historical events and consequences, basin characteristics, topography, hydrology and meteorology, geology and geomorphology, land cover/use, settlements, critical infrastructure, ecological vulnerability, cultural heritage, climate information and spatial-development plans. Flood types include fluvial, urban/pluvial, groundwater, coastal and hydraulic-infrastructure failure; mudflows and glacial-lake outbursts may also be considered.

The decree requires the methodology to be approved by 1 June 2027 and the first national preliminary flood-risk assessment to be completed by 31 December 2028. Assessments are reviewed at intervals no longer than six years.

## INFERENCE

This is a formal national discovery and prioritization framework, not proof that all significant-risk areas have already been identified or mapped. Its outputs are intended to support later hazard maps, risk maps and risk-management measures.

## HYPOTHESIS

If the first national assessment combines complete historical event records with consistent basin, exposure and critical-infrastructure data, then it may improve prioritization of areas for detailed hazard/risk mapping and subsequent warning/response planning.

## UNTESTED IDEA
