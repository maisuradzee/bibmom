# NOEMA-2026-013 — Nitrate health evidence and drinking-water threshold provenance

**Date:** 2026-11-28  
**Stream:** AquaSignal / NOEMA-2026-006  
**Status:** Preliminary — not independently peer reviewed  
**Evidence boundary:** public-source review and synthetic software tests only; no Georgian sample-level data were accessed.

## Research question

How should AquaSignal surface emerging evidence about chronic low-level nitrate exposure without conflating (1) an international health-based guideline, (2) a Georgian regulatory text, (3) a foreign expert recommendation, (4) observational epidemiology, and (5) advocacy or policy signals?

## Executive finding

A new 2026 New Zealand national retrospective cohort study reports an association between estimated drinking-water nitrate exposure and preterm birth across 735,831 singleton births. Its exposure is reported as **mg/L nitrate-nitrogen (NO3-N)**. The Danish expert report recommending 6 mg/L nitrate in drinking water was revised on 2026-01-07 after correcting confusion between nitrate ion (NO3-) and nitrate-nitrogen (NO3-N); it explicitly says only one study was suitable for its benchmark-dose modelling. The report's 6 mg/L is an expert recommendation, not evidence in this review of an enacted Danish legal limit.

The WHO's 2026 consolidated drinking-water volume still gives 50 mg/L as nitrate ion, but its nitrate/nitrite fact sheet states an assessment date of 2016. Georgia's Government Decree №58/2014 separately lists 50 mg/L as NO3- for nitrate and 0.2 mg/L as NO2- for nitrite, with a scope gate and different limiting signs. These references are not interchangeable.

The practical conclusion is not that Georgia's current standard is unsafe or that a new threshold has been established. It is that AquaSignal must keep **legal compliance**, **international guidance**, **expert recommendations**, and **emerging health evidence** in separate, source-versioned channels.

## Evidence ledger

### FACT — WHO drinking-water guidance

The WHO's consolidated *Guidelines for drinking-water quality: fourth edition incorporating the first, second and third addenda* was published on 2026-06-17. Its nitrate/nitrite chemical fact sheet gives 50 mg/L as nitrate ion (NO3-) and 3 mg/L as nitrite ion (NO2-), with the combined ratio rule when both occur in the same drinking-water sample. The fact sheet's assessment date is 2016. The 2026 consolidated publication date must not be represented as a 2026 nitrate reassessment.

Source: [WHO publication and chemical fact sheets](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240121225).

### FACT — Georgian regulatory text

Government Decree №58/2014, within its Article 1 scope, lists nitrate at 50 mg/L as NO3- with a short-term exposure limiting sign and nitrite at 0.2 mg/L as NO2- with a long-term exposure limiting sign. Article 1 includes scope exclusions. The table values therefore cannot be applied automatically to raw environmental groundwater or to every private supply. Currentness, amendments and supply-specific scope remain legal-review items.

Source: [Matsne — Government Decree №58/2014](https://www.matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/2196792).

### FACT — Danish expert report and unit correction

The Danish Ministry's international expert group report, *Evaluation of the parametric value for Nitrate in drinking water*, was delivered on 2025-11-24 and the corrected version dated 2026-01-07. The report says the earlier version was refined because some underlying studies reported nitrate ion (NO3-) while others reported nitrate-nitrogen (NO3-N), and the distinction had not been applied consistently. The corrected report supersedes the original.

The group recommends 6 mg/L nitrate in drinking water as a precautionary parametric value. It explains that the recommendation is grounded in two Danish population-based studies, but only one study was considered suitable for the benchmark-dose modelling; that study supplied five exposure categories. The report explicitly discusses the limited number of studies and modelling uncertainty. This is an expert recommendation to a ministry, not by itself an enacted legal limit.

Sources:
- [Corrected Danish expert report, 7 January 2026](https://mim.dk/media/o4hptfht/nitratereport-jan-7-2026-revised-clean.pdf).
- [Danish Ministry publication page](https://mim.dk/publikationer/2025/december/evaluation-of-the-parametric-value-for-nitrate-in-drinking-water).

### FACT — new peer-reviewed New Zealand cohort

Chambers et al. (2026), *Nitrate in drinking water and preterm birth: a national retrospective cohort study in New Zealand*, analysed linked administrative health data for 735,831 live singleton births from 2008-01-01 to 2021-12-31. Prenatal exposure from public and domestic self-supplied drinking water was expressed as mg/L nitrate-nitrogen (NO3-N).

The paper reports:
- 5.7% of the cohort births were preterm;
- adjusted odds ratio 1.01 (95% CI 1.01–1.02) per 1 mg/L increase in NO3-N for all preterm birth;
- highest versus lowest exposure quartile adjusted odds ratio 1.08 (95% CI 1.05–1.12) for all preterm birth;
- highest versus lowest quartile adjusted odds ratio 1.17 (95% CI 1.03–1.32) for extremely early preterm birth;
- an estimated population attributable fraction of about 120 preterm births per year / 4% **only under a causal assumption**.

The authors state that residual confounding and exposure misclassification cannot be excluded. This is an observational association, not proof of causation, an individual risk prediction, or a threshold derivation. The study was not independently reproduced by NOEMA in this run.

Source: [PubMed record, PMID 42385869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42385869/) — DOI [10.1016/j.envres.2026.125169](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envres.2026.125169).

### FACT — policy and advocacy signals, not new standards

Greenpeace International reported on 2026-11-03 that more than 80 health, environment and food advocacy organisations had issued an open letter asking WHO to prioritise a full nitrate review in the next guideline revision. This is a documented advocacy signal; it is not a WHO decision or a revised guideline.

Source: [Greenpeace International, 3 November 2026](https://www.greenpeace.org/international/press-release/86816/who-nitrate-drinking-water-guidelines-health-risks/).

The Danish Parliament's question 199 (submitted 2026-10-21) asks whether the minister will request a full WHO reassessment of nitrate in the fifth edition. The official page records a final ministerial answer dated 2026-11-16. The answer PDF's substantive content was not retrieved and reviewed in this run, so no conclusion is drawn about what the minister agreed to do.

Source: [Folketing — MOF question 199 and final answer metadata](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20261/almdel/MOF/spm/199/svar/2254457/index.htm).

## Unit and matrix comparability

| Source | Value | Native basis | Matrix / scope | Evidence type |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| WHO 2026 consolidated volume | 50 mg/L | NO3- | Drinking water | International guideline; fact sheet assessment 2016 |
| Georgia Decree №58/2014 | 50 mg/L | NO3- | Drinking water only after Article 1 scope check | Regulatory text; legal scope/currentness review required |
| Danish expert group | 6 mg/L | NO3- | Drinking water | Expert recommendation; not treated as enacted law |
| Chambers et al. 2026 | Continuous exposure and quartiles | NO3-N | Estimated drinking-water exposure in New Zealand cohort | Observational study result, not a threshold |

Do not compare a NO3-N result directly with a NO3- value. The conversion factor published by WHO is rounded; do not use a rounded conversion as the sole basis for a near-threshold legal decision. Do not apply drinking-water references to raw groundwater unless the sample's intended use and applicable legal matrix have been established. No Georgian sample data, exposure distribution, prevalence, or compliance result is calculated here.

## Evidence classification

- **FACT:** the cited WHO, Georgian legal, Danish expert-report, New Zealand cohort and parliamentary/advocacy source records say what is summarised above.
- **REPRODUCED_RESULT:** none. NOEMA did not reproduce the New Zealand cohort analysis or the Danish benchmark-dose model.
- **INFERENCE:** the newer New Zealand cohort post-dates the Danish report's corrected 2026-01-07 version and is therefore additional evidence for a future review; it does not retroactively invalidate or validate the Danish recommendation.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** a dual-channel AquaSignal design (legal-control channel plus emerging-evidence watch channel) can reduce threshold conflation while keeping operators informed of new evidence.
- **UNTESTED IDEA:** use a separate, non-alerting research watch to flag new peer-reviewed studies and official policy changes for human review; it has not been evaluated for sensitivity, specificity or operational value.

## Innovation — benchmark provenance guard

Added noema_core/nitrate_benchmarks.py. It permits contextual comparison only when analyte basis and water matrix match exactly. It distinguishes international guideline, national regulatory text and expert recommendation. It deliberately rejects peer-reviewed studies and advocacy signals as numeric thresholds, performs no automatic unit conversion, and always returns compliance_decision_permitted=false and potability_decision_permitted=false.

This is a synthetic, deterministic helper, not a production alert engine. It does not decide whether a supply is legally in scope, whether a regulation is current, or whether water is safe.

## Methodology

1. Re-opened primary WHO, Matsne and Danish Ministry documents.
2. Read the PubMed bibliographic record and abstract for the 2026 New Zealand cohort.
3. Recorded the Greenpeace release only as an advocacy/policy signal and the Danish parliamentary page only as proof that a final answer was issued; the answer content was not inspected.
4. Preserved each source's native nitrate basis and drinking-water matrix.
5. Added closed JSON Schema validation and synthetic unit tests for benchmark provenance, matrix/basis mismatch and prohibited compliance conclusions.
